Why hand-rolled leaves holes

You cannot bolt causality onto facts you already discarded.

Teams can add action logs, undo stacks, entity adapters, persistence middleware, framework wrappers, and AI audit prose one at a time. The holes appear where those systems disagree about identity, authority, and when an operation is complete.

intent authority turn boundary subject identity snapshot

Two histories can produce one snapshot. Therefore the snapshot cannot uniquely determine its history. The facts must be captured while the operation is happening.

The honest competitive claim

Can another library do this?

Yes—by adopting the same class of responsibilities.

A team can build stable identity, causal turns, external authority, coherent publication, effect-based restoration, lifecycle ownership, and native framework adapters around another state container. But when those parts share one authority and one settlement model, the team has built a causal state kernel around it.

That is the defensible difference: SignalTree ships the integrated model. Hand-rolled alternatives pay for it repeatedly in glue code—or leave gaps that only appear during undo, synchronization, SSR, migration, incident response, and autonomous execution.

SignalTree does not claim that causal software is impossible elsewhere. It makes causal integrity the default architecture instead of an application-wide reconstruction project.

The cost you cannot query from a snapshot

What don't you know?

A dashboard can be green while its authority is stale. A payment can validate while its intent is wrong. A retry can succeed twice. Keep the facts that let people and agents tell the difference. SignalTree preserves that evidence; application policy and human review still decide what to do.
