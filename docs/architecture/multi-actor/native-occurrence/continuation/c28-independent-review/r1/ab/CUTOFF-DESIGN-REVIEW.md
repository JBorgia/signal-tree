This review concerns the design, not an unseen implementation.

The P/S/C/L distinction is sound for the observed failure. A/B has closed prior P at both fields; x retains old S, while y selects new C. Readiness of y's C is not historical closure. Requiring genuine P, any distinct S, and contributing consumed historical requirements to be closed conservatively avoids projecting same-identity facts out of unresolved history. Do not discover eligibility at settlement or add newly confirmed owners later. Captured-pending owners remain protected even after confirmation. Declining cleanup while old evidence is open is a bounded eligibility restriction, not a new promise about intermediate receipts.

H and V may share a prospective projection mask only under the explicit conditions in the design. This does not make their authority identical: H classifies closed historical I/V; V classifies already-confirmed invocation I/V for publication relevance AFTER complete raw validation. In A/B, a is freshly named validation, not merely historical debris. Its authenticity and nested completeness must still be checked. Its irrelevance to fresh no-write after validation is already owner-approved. Pending, rejected/abandoned, unavailable, D/W and unclassified actual roles and flags remain protected. A mask must never become independent-declaration permission or suppress raw installedOwners/frame checks.

Preserving raw C and issued inputs, projecting only a fresh transported certificate, and comparing the actual reduced aggregate prior/effective values addresses the demonstrated distinction without rewriting old receipts or guessing that a leaf predecessor equals recipient prior. Source normalization and comparison remain conceptually separate even if they reuse a mask after all conditions hold. The prior aggregate can already reflect an earlier sibling reduction; the design correctly retains ordinary occurrence replacement and noWrite handling.

Concrete implementation obligations before acceptance:

1. Enumerate the historical closure inputs explicitly. Do not limit the check to P while a distinct S or consumed requirement can contribute a masked identity; do not recursively normalize an issued input as a shortcut. Preserve exact restoration/literal bypasses. Admission eligibility should be immutable and should not retain P/history indefinitely.
2. Enforce the timing barrier on every projection consumer. Frozen combineScalarNativeRequirements currently exposes a projected getter immediately (lifecycle.ts:186–191). Frozen finish() also projects recipient op.proof at approximately 446, refreshes/wakes operations, then invokes readiness listeners. Changing only the source getter or normalization callback does not prove recipient projection waits for activation. The successor must explicitly establish the intended ordering/readiness at these sites, plus private ready() and projectRevision(). Source-reading this schedule is not proof that an early send already occurs.
3. Preserve errors while closing horizons. Ready means finalization AND activation, not successful publication permission. A callback/validation/wake failure may still run cleanup/completion; invalid/rejected/unknown flags and primary thrown identity must survive. Interpret the design's “successfully” barrier as no permission to bless invalid proof, not permission to skip cleanup after failure. No new error-precedence policy.
4. Compute retirement classification from unprojected constituents before deleting ancestry. Shorter publication roles cannot change static retirement blockers or exact qualifying-window identities. Normalize only the new certificate; old supplied snapshots, tickets and earlier receipt dependencies retain their issued meaning.
5. Release transient closure/mask edges after normalization/operation completion. Flat visible proof alone is not evidence that removed owner cells or predecessor certificates are no longer retained. Keep separate source, recipient, active-operation and issued-evidence counts; protect real active older receipt obligations.

The proposed six finite discriminator groups are appropriate. Mandatory witnesses are unchanged A/B with earlier receipt sending once, open genuine old contributor with no premature cleanup, pending-at-capture then confirmed, nested confirmed-validator contradiction, opaque dual obligations and saved-before-q isolation, and both activation/finalization orders with thrown-sentinel and retirement controls. No new whole-suite run is required for design eligibility. The barrier-order case above is the cheapest additional implementation discriminator if the successor changes only one of the projection paths.

No blocker to beginning the bounded prototype is demonstrated. Acceptance requires the actual delta to establish these conditions; neither the original five horizon passes nor the design alone qualifies the new deferral mechanism.
