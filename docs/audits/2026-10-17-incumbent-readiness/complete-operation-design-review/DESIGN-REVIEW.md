## Refinement 1: preserve relationship-relative baselines explicitly

DESIGN.md lines 90–92 and 122 discuss eligible ordinary projection and agreement among consumers. The qualification that different projections are permitted helps, but does not name a load-bearing exception: **a Link relationship's initial baseline may legitimately include inspection state that is absent from source ordinary state**. The same selected inverse can therefore leave different Links with different final payloads without either consumer contradicting the source transition.

Production `link.ts`'s relationship-creation adoption paragraph (around lines 311–323; f01 lines 355–365) explicitly establishes that baseline. The candidate's `ordinary-order-source-boundaries.spec.ts:102–132` requires an older Link to end at `[a1]` and a Link created after inspection to end at `[x9,a1]` after the same selected field rollback. Lines 58–94 additionally require ordinary removal to affect an inspection-created member in the later relationship baseline, even though that member was absent from source ordinary state.

Required clarification: the composer supplies scoped authored/realized deltas and dependencies; each Link applies the authorized footprint to its relationship baseline. “Same completed facts” does not mean reseeding every Link from a common ordinary endpoint or canonical source snapshot. State that relationships are part of the relevant projection context when comparing endpoint consistency. Include connection creation during divergence as an explicit acceptance row, rather than relying on the generic multiple-connections item.

Cheapest falsifier: retain the two named ordinary-order-source-boundaries cases, plus the existing creation-adoption controls, unchanged for any successor. **Not run in this design review.** The source and assertions demonstrate why the requirement is necessary; they do not establish that a future composer satisfies it.

The alternatives table contrasts sparse completed units with per-scope snapshots and independently extended arrays. It omits a viable intermediate alternative: **extend the existing shared boundary/effect representation with paired ordinary continuity and complete-domain composition, reusing existing capture/retention rather than adding another retained sequence of complete records**.

F01 also already retains transaction-local `OperationRecord` chains (`transactions.ts:2263–2277`) and folds them separately (`2571–2590`), with explicit terminal cleanup. A proposal must explain whether its completed-unit representation replaces, enriches, or duplicates those obligations. “Sparse units” alone does not answer that question.

Compare shared paired boundary extension against sparse retained unit records on the same semantic cases, foreign barriers, retention owners and work bounds. If the former cannot preserve reentry identity, ordinary-only adoption or selected inverse dependencies, identify the minimal counterexample. If they are merely two layouts of the same sufficient facts, say so instead of claiming an architectural winner. Snapshot reference controls can retain separate authority/dependency metadata and can be lazily materialized for full-order work; the weakest unconditional snapshot design should not be the only comparator.

Cheapest falsifier: a static producer/consumer/retention mapping showing a required fact that the shared boundary representation cannot retain without duplication or semantic loss. **Not yet supplied; no model executed.** This omission does not invalidate the draft as an inventory, but blocks claiming the alternatives have selected a representation.
