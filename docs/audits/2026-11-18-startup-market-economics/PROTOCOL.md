## Runtime investigation

1. Inspect source, history and deliberate behavior before changing it. Attribute
   construction, initial population, first read/activation, first edit and cleanup
   separately on identified built artifacts. CPU samples guide hypotheses; they
   do not establish exact additive causal percentages.
2. Independently challenge which work must occur immediately and which may be
   deferred. No new weaker semantic mode or public configuration is assumed.
   An optional import, dormant participant and public feature switch are different
   mechanisms. Demand activation must preserve writes preceding attachment.
3. Prototype the largest defensible mechanisms. Compare each release line to its
   own frozen current build, including same-artifact controls. Preserve rejected
   candidates and adverse results. Stable-v15 compatibility remains binding.
4. Exercise late readers, held writers, foreign owners, supplied inputs, entity
   replacement, reentry, refusal/recovery, retirement and actual teardown as
   applicable. Preserve the existing v16 point-work bound; do not replace its
   threshold. Positive useful work and planted-defect sensitivity are required.
5. Measure deferred cost where it is paid: first activation, first useful edit,
   full context, pending pressure and repeated store lifetimes. Factory savings
   alone do not establish a lifetime win. Separate timing and forced-GC children.
6. Qualify selected changes on fresh packages with the relevant full gate set,
   exact restoration, installed consumers, production demo and documentation
   gates. Numeric misses remain visible and require their own cost disposition.

## Customer and supplier economics

Use current primary sources with retrieval dates and exact links for software
pricing, compensation and industry profitability. Distinguish absolute industry
profits, margin, ability to pay and product fit. Prioritize the cost of relevant
workflows and mistakes rather than assuming the richest industry is the best
buyer. State geography, currency and periods; default scenarios are US/USD.

Build a reproducible sensitivity model with explicit inputs and formulas:

- Customer: development/maintenance effort avoided, incident investigation time,
  review burden, integration/migration/training, operation/retention costs and
  measured application-performance penalties where a defensible valuation exists.
- Risk: only attributable preventable losses, explicit frequency/effectiveness
  assumptions, no claim that SignalTree prevents all AI incidents. Keep speculative
  risk savings separate from the labor-only case and avoid double counting.
- Pricing: public price anchors and modeled affordable ceilings. Willingness to
  pay is unobserved without buyers; scenarios are hypotheses, not research results.
  Keep free public runtime economics separate from potential paid private tooling,
  support, hosted investigation and enterprise integration.
- Supplier: paid accounts, seat/account mix, annual revenue, hosting/support cost,
  gross margin, customer acquisition, churn/replacement and fixed operating costs.
  Revenue is not profit. Scale cases are conditional adoption counts, not forecasts.
- Broad adoption: calculate aggregate potential savings and resource costs only
  for explicitly eligible deployments. Do not convert a global AI market estimate
  into an addressable market without a supported eligibility chain.

Report break-even hours/incidents, payback, cost per user/action where meaningful,
negative-value cases and sensitivity ranges. No fractional-second delay is treated
as fully recoverable employee wages without an explicit blocking/recovery factor.
Do not claim clinical, financial, compliance or security certification.
