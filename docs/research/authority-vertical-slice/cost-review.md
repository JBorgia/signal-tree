## Required interpretation corrections

1. Attribute CPU differences to these implementations, not the authority premise. A deep-clones the entire entity envelope/payload twice per write (canonical authored fact then native projection), and once per settlement; B clones once on ingestion, then shares internally detached facts with native storage and settlement. Both clone public return values. For ordinary cycles A counts 8 prepared payload object nodes plus 2 read payload nodes per cycle; B counts 6 total payload object nodes. For transaction cycles A counts 6 prepared plus 2 read nodes; B counts 4 total. These extra copies are concrete A-model costs, but an independent authority design need not deep-copy its internal immutable-by-discipline facts into projections. A also retains empty pending Maps/location records for ordinary present locations. The experiment does not isolate unavoidable authority overhead from these representation choices.

2. Do not compare raw `publications`, `visitedLocations`, or aggregate allocations as common units. A publications counts callback invocations; B counts install requests. A visitedLocations counts affected locations while resolutionVisits separately counts pending contributions. B visitedLocations includes both. For these workloads A visitedLocations + resolutionVisits equals B visitedLocations (ordinary 2, transaction/observed 3, overlap 8 per cycle). Use installedUnits, nativeWrites and observerDeliveries as common units, and spell out each copy category. A namedModelObjects and B selected allocation counters are incomplete and differently scoped logical inventories, not allocated bytes or total allocations.

3. CPU and wall figures include assertions, reads, counter instrumentation, clone-node traversal, async cycle/flush scheduling and error-array checks. A flush is async and yields microtasks; B flush synchronously drains. Their notification algorithms also differ: A coalesces queued notifications, B queues one observer batch per installation. Timed observed cycles flush after every installation, making delivery counts equal for this workload without establishing equivalent scheduling generally. Three short samples with fixed scenario order and alternating arm order support descriptive medians/ranges only, not stable production ratios or statistical superiority.

4. Control timing includes substantial extra translation/runtime work. Its flush includes setImmediate, whereas models do not. Its observer shim calls store.snapshot() before invoking the callback even though the timed callback ignores that argument; this enumerates all entity rows and builds wrappers/output on every observed delivery. Its lifecycle queries, real enhancement/runtime behavior and flat native replaceOne path also differ from the neutral models. Keep control numbers explicitly labeled whole adapted workload; do not claim that the control/model timing gap measures authority cost. The absence of control overlap timing is a scope exclusion, not a zero-cost observation.

5. Heap figures are whole-process heapUsed bytes after forced GC, not store retained sizes or allocation rates. seededHeap-emptyHeap includes seeding/runtime effects; beforeHeap is sampled after warm-up/resetStats and afterHeap after the loop plus GC. Negative loopHeapDelta values are present and consistent with collection/JIT/runtime variation; they are not negative allocations. afterHeap includes loaded module/runtime baselines. destroyedHeap still has the store and closure references alive, so it cannot establish full collection or leak freedom. Logical terminal obligation counts at zero establish only counted model bookkeeping reclamation. Report heap ranges and these limits, and do not infer pending-memory peaks because the harness samples only after all owners settle.

## Bounded conclusion

The saved summaries faithfully describe the supplied passing workloads. B has lower measured CPU medians in these samples and lower seeded whole-process heap deltas, but different copying, bookkeeping and scheduling implementations prevent attribution to authority alone. Neither model passes the entire expanded contract; neither timings nor retained-count observations select an architectural winner or establish production superiority. No rerun is necessary to report these narrowly bounded facts. A controlled copying/scheduling variant and fuller semantics would be needed for stronger causal claims.
